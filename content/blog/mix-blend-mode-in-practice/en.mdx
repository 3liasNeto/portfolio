`mix-blend-mode` defines how an element blends with whatever is **behind** it — just like Photoshop layers. It's one line of CSS that unlocks effects many people would build with canvas.

## The three modes I use the most

- **`difference`**: inverts the background colors. Perfect for cursors and text that must stay readable over anything.
- **`color-dodge`**: brightens and saturates. It's the foundation of holographic effects.
- **`multiply`**: darkens, like ink on paper. Great for textures.

## A cursor that always shows up

```css
.cursor {
  position: fixed;
  width: 40px;
  height: 40px;
  border-radius: 50%;
  background: white;
  mix-blend-mode: difference;
  pointer-events: none;
}
```

With a white background and `difference`, the circle turns black over light areas and white over dark ones. No logic required.

<Callout type="warning">
  Blending only sees what's in the same **stacking context**. An `isolation: isolate` (or a `transform`) on the parent can make the effect "disappear".
</Callout>

## Performance

Blend modes are computed on the GPU and are usually cheap. What hurts is animating `width`, `top` or `left`: prefer `transform` and `translate`, which don't trigger layout.
